Method

How a rating becomes a prediction

01

Collect the season

Every completed game is pulled from the college football and basketball data APIs: final scores, schedule, venue and, where posted, the market line. Football rates FBS and FCS teams on the same scale, because a large share of FBS games are played against FCS opponents and those results only mean something if both sides carry a real rating.

02

Fit a rating to each team

A ridge regression fits one rating per team to the season's scoring margins, so a team's number reflects who it played as well as by how much it won. Early in the season there is little to fit on, so the regression is pulled toward a preseason projection built from the team's own final rating last season and a published preseason rating.

As games accumulate, the weight on this season's results grows and the prior fades.

03

Project the game

The difference between two ratings, plus home advantage where it applies, is the projected margin. That margin converts to a win probability for each side.

Where a sportsbook has posted a line, the model's number is compared against it. The gap between the two is the edge — not a claim that the model is right, but a measure of where it disagrees most with the market.
