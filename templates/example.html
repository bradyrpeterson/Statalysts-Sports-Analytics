Guide

How to read
a prediction.

This is the real predictions board with invented games in it, so you can see exactly what the page looks like — including out of season, when there is nothing live to show. Every number below is made up. Nothing on this page is a real projection.

Column 1

Matchup

Away team on top, home team below, with each team's record this season. The side the model favors is set in white; the other is dimmed. Kickoff and neutral-site status sit underneath.

Column 2

Model line

The model's own projected margin. “Ohio State −9.5” means it expects Ohio State to win by nine and a half points. This comes from the difference between the two power ratings, plus home advantage.

Column 3

Market line

The sportsbook spread for the same game, shown for comparison. A dash means no line has been posted yet — which is common early in the week.

Column 4

Edge

The gap between those two numbers. A large edge means the model and the market disagree sharply about the game. It is a measure of disagreement, not a measure of confidence, and certainly not a guarantee — the market is right more often than not.

Column 5

Win probability

The model's estimated chance that its projected winner actually wins outright. Click any row to open the full breakdown, including the split between the two teams and the venue.
